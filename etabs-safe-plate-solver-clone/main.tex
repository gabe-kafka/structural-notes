\section{Purpose}

Define a general finite-element plate solver for slab behavior.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries PRIMARY OUTPUTS}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7in}p{4.8in}@{}}
Displacements & nodal $w$, $\theta_x$, $\theta_y$ or selected plate DOFs \\
Reactions & support reactions at constrained DOFs and support regions \\
Support actions & resultants such as $V$, $M_x$, $M_y$ over finite supports \\
Fields & optional moments, shears, stresses, deflections, contours \\
Diagnostics & convergence, equilibrium, mesh quality, support resolution \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Consumers}

The solver is not a punching-shear solver. Punching shear is the first consumer
of support actions.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries DOWNSTREAM USES}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8in}p{4.7in}@{}}
Punching shear & consume $V_u,M_{ux},M_{uy}$ and run critical-section $J_c$ check \\
Slab transfer & integrate column/wall reactions and support eccentricity \\
Deflection & report service displacement fields and envelopes \\
Validation & compare support reactions, deflections, and moment fields to references \\
Design workflows & future strip forces, envelopes, and reinforcement design inputs \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Reference Target}

ETABS/SAFE-style slab analysis is the validation target. The goal is not to
copy private implementation details, but to match the public engineering
behavior closely enough that benchmark models produce the same reactions,
support moments, deflections, and downstream punching demands within documented
tolerances.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries TARGET BEHAVIOR}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8in}p{4.7in}@{}}
Shell/plate model & slab represented by meshed plate/shell finite elements \\
Object supports & point, line, wall, column, spring, and area/patch supports \\
Rigid zones & finite column/support regions avoid point-singularity behavior \\
Load cases & solve individual cases and factored combinations explicitly \\
Postprocessing & recover support actions, fields, envelopes, and code-check inputs \\
\end{tabular}
}

Benchmark success means the same physical model, loading, supports, and mesh
intent give matching results against ETABS/SAFE, not merely internal
equilibrium.

\section{Development Reality}

A useful solver kernel can be built before ETABS/SAFE automation exists, but it
cannot be accepted as an ETABS/SAFE clone until benchmark comparison is running.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries LOCAL WORK WITHOUT CSI}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.0in}p{4.5in}@{}}
Element unit tests & stiffness symmetry, rigid-body modes, patch tests \\
Load-vector tests & applied load sums and consistent nodal loading \\
Constraint tests & point, line, spring, patch, and settlement mapping \\
Equilibrium tests & recovered reactions balance applied load \\
Closed-form checks & simple square/strip plate deflection benchmarks \\
Symmetry checks & mirrored models produce mirrored reactions and moments \\
\end{tabular}
}

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries REQUIRES ETABS/SAFE}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.0in}p{4.5in}@{}}
Benchmark parity & reactions, moments, deflections, and field results vs CSI \\
Support tuning & column rigid zones, wall supports, springs, patch behavior \\
Meshing tolerance & how fine the mesh must be for CSI-like support actions \\
Punching demands & $V_u,M_{ux},M_{uy}$ matching SAFE-style punching inputs \\
Production acceptance & release only after fixture set passes tolerances \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Tuning Philosophy}

Treat ETABS/SAFE as the reference oracle for practical two-way slab behavior.
Tune only documented modeling choices, not arbitrary correction factors.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries TUNABLE MODELING CHOICES}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.1in}p{4.4in}@{}}
Element formulation & DKT vs Mindlin/Reissner or later mixed element \\
Support representation & point, line, spring, rigid patch, elastic patch \\
Rigid zone extent & finite column/support footprint behavior \\
Mesh strategy & object-based meshing, local refinement, element shape limits \\
Result smoothing & raw element fields vs averaged display/design fields \\
Load application & area, line, point, and combination handling \\
\end{tabular}
}

Do not tune by applying hidden multipliers to results. If a benchmark does not
match, first identify whether the cause is element formulation, support model,
mesh, load case definition, units, or result recovery.

\section{Model Inputs}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7in}p{4.8in}@{}}
Geometry & slab polygon, openings, levels, thickness regions \\
Material & $E_c$, $\nu$, density, cracking modifiers if used \\
Loads & area, line, point, thermal, settlement, load cases and combinations \\
Supports & point pins, point springs, line/wall supports, finite patches, rotational springs \\
Mesh controls & target edge length, local refinement, support footprint resolution \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Coordinate System}

\begin{equation}
x,y = \text{slab plan axes}, \qquad z = \text{vertical}
\end{equation}

Use one explicit sign convention throughout the solver.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries PROPOSED CONVENTION}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7in}p{4.8in}@{}}
$w$ & positive downward vertical displacement \\
$q$ & positive downward pressure \\
$V$ & positive downward force transferred from slab to support \\
$M_x,M_y$ & right-hand-rule support moments about global axes, with signs documented \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Element Formulation}

Open decision: choose the production plate element.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries CANDIDATES}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.7in}p{4.8in}@{}}
DKT triangle & thin-plate bending, three DOFs per node, good for Kirchhoff plates \\
Mindlin/Reissner & includes transverse shear, needs locking control for thin slabs \\
Mixed formulation & possible future route for shear forces and thick/slender robustness \\
\end{tabular}
}

For the first solver contract, only require that each element provides:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf K_e,\qquad \mathbf f_e,\qquad \text{element recovery operators}
\end{equation}

\section{Load Vector}

Convert loads into consistent element or nodal load vectors.

\begin{equation}
\mathbf f_e = \int_{A_e} \mathbf N^T q\,dA
\end{equation}

For a simple triangular element with linear vertical displacement interpolation:

\begin{equation}
F_{w,i}^{(e)} = \frac{q A_e}{3}
\end{equation}

Open item: define how rotational load terms are handled for the chosen plate
element.

\section{Support Types}

The solver must support multiple boundary-condition families.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries SUPPORT CATALOG}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8in}p{4.7in}@{}}
Point pin & constrain $w$ at a node; rotations free \\
Point fixed & constrain $w,\theta_x,\theta_y$ at a node \\
Point spring & add translational and/or rotational spring stiffness \\
Line/wall support & constrain or spring nodes along a wall/polyline \\
Finite patch & support over a column, bearing plate, wall end, or reaction area \\
Rigid patch & finite patch with master-slave rigid vertical kinematics \\
Elastic foundation & distributed Winkler/Pasternak style support over an area \\
Settlement & prescribed support displacement at constrained or spring-supported DOFs \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Point and Line Supports}

Pinned point support:

\begin{equation}
w_i = 0
\end{equation}

Rotationally fixed point support:

\begin{equation}
w_i=0,\qquad \theta_{x,i}=0,\qquad \theta_{y,i}=0
\end{equation}

Spring support:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf K \leftarrow \mathbf K + \mathbf K_s
\end{equation}

Line supports are modeled by applying compatible point constraints or springs to
mesh nodes on the support line, or by a distributed support element.

\section{Finite Support Patches}

Finite patches are required when the support footprint matters.

\medskip{\ttfamily\footnotesize\bfseries EXAMPLES}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8in}p{4.7in}@{}}
Column footprint & rectangular or polygonal area around column \\
Bearing plate & finite support under equipment or transfer load \\
Wall strip & finite-width wall support, not just a line \\
Odd geometry & clipped or rotated reaction areas \\
\end{tabular}
}

Mesh requirements:

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8in}p{4.7in}@{}}
Master node & at patch centroid when rigid patch is used \\
Slave nodes & enough mesh nodes inside patch to avoid point-support behavior \\
Refinement & target edge tied to support size, not only slab span \\
Diagnostics & slave count, master offset, patch area, clipped footprint \\
\end{tabular}
}

\section{Rigid Patch Kinematics}

For rigid vertical patch behavior:

\begin{equation}
w_s = w_c + \theta_y \Delta x - \theta_x \Delta y
\end{equation}

Transformation form:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf u = \mathbf T\mathbf q,\qquad
\mathbf K_r = \mathbf T^T\mathbf K\mathbf T,\qquad
\mathbf F_r = \mathbf T^T\mathbf F
\end{equation}

Open item: decide when a rigid patch is mandatory versus optional.

\section{Assembly and Solve}

Assemble:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf K = \sum_e \mathbf A_e^T\mathbf K_e\mathbf A_e,\qquad
\mathbf F = \sum_e \mathbf A_e^T\mathbf f_e
\end{equation}

Apply constraints and solve:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf K_r\mathbf q = \mathbf F_r
\end{equation}

Then expand to full DOFs:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf u = \mathbf T\mathbf q
\end{equation}

\section{Reaction Recovery}

At a constrained DOF:

\begin{equation}
R_i = (\mathbf K\mathbf u)_i - F_i
\end{equation}

Demand on the support is the opposite sign:

\begin{equation}
P_i = F_i - (\mathbf K\mathbf u)_i
\end{equation}

For springs, the support demand can also be recovered from spring deformation:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf P_s = \mathbf K_s(\mathbf u-\mathbf u_0)
\end{equation}

\section{Support Resultants}

For any finite support region with vertical demand samples $P_i$:

\begin{align}
V &= \sum_i P_i \\
M_x &= \sum_i (y_i-y_c)P_i \\
M_y &= -\sum_i (x_i-x_c)P_i
\end{align}

Equivalent eccentricity form:

\begin{align}
x_R &= \frac{\sum_i x_iP_i}{V}, \qquad
y_R = \frac{\sum_i y_iP_i}{V} \\
e_x &= x_R-x_c,\qquad e_y=y_R-y_c \\
M_x &= V e_y,\qquad M_y=-V e_x
\end{align}

These resultants are general. Punching shear is one workflow that consumes them.

\section{Local Axes}

For a support patch rotated by $\theta$:

\begin{align}
M_1 &= M_x\cos\theta + M_y\sin\theta \\
M_2 &= -M_x\sin\theta + M_y\cos\theta
\end{align}

Open item: define local-axis convention once and reuse it for columns, walls,
loads, and punching checks.

\section{Check Order}

{\ttfamily\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.8in}p{5.9in}@{}}
1 & Build plate geometry, materials, loads, supports, and mesh controls \\
2 & Mesh slab with refinement near supports, openings, and result regions \\
3 & Assemble element stiffness and load vectors \\
4 & Apply point, line, spring, patch, settlement, and rigid constraints \\
5 & Solve reduced plate system \\
6 & Expand full displacement vector \\
7 & Recover reactions, support resultants, and field quantities \\
8 & Run diagnostics and mark each recovered result stable/degraded/unstable \\
9 & Compare benchmark fixtures against ETABS/SAFE exports before production use \\
10 & Pass accepted recovered artifacts to downstream workflows \\
\end{tabular}
}
